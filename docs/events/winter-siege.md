# Winter Siege

::: tip
**Quick take:** Monthly alliance-vs-alliance Stronghold battle. Top 20 alliances in your state, Furnace Lv. 25 personal minimum. Each Chief deploys up to two squads on Wednesday and Thursday, everything locks Thursday 23:59 UTC, and Saturday is a single 24-hour Battle Phase. Troops are provided by the system and nothing dies permanently. The whole event is a Star count: your garrisoned squads carry Stars, and you only take an enemy's Stars by destroying a squad completely. Partial damage scores nothing.
:::

::: warning Rewritten September 2026
This page previously published a Morale decay schedule with a **35% floor**, **3 Hearts** per normal squad, **30**-squad garrison capacity, and a **four-Stronghold** map. All four were wrong or have since changed. The current figures are below. If you have a copy of the older guidance, discard it — the Morale floor in particular changes which enemy squads are worth attacking at all.
:::

## At a Glance

- **Frequency:** Monthly, starting the Monday of Alliance Showdown week
- **Duration:** Seven-day cycle, one 24-hour Battle Phase on Saturday
- **Eligibility:** Alliance must be top 20 by power in the state. Each Chief needs Furnace Lv. 25 and must be in the alliance when matchmaking begins
- **Troop cost:** None. Troops are system-provided and all injuries recover after each battle
- **Why it matters:** Tier rewards, Stronghold Conquest Rewards claimable win or lose, and improved Win Streak rewards for two consecutive victories

## How It Works

Two alliances are matched against each other. Each holds a set of Strongholds. Your members garrison squads in your own Strongholds during the Preparation Phase; on Saturday, those same squads defend automatically and can also be sent to attack the enemy's Strongholds.

**The three numbers that run the event:**

| Icon | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Flag** | The number of squads garrisoned in a Stronghold, against its capacity |
| **Star** | Score. Every garrisoned squad contributes Stars to the Stronghold it sits in. The amount varies by Stronghold **type** |
| **Heart** | A squad's health. A squad loses one Heart every time it loses a defensive battle |

**The rule that shapes everything else:** a squad's Stars are only deducted when it loses **all** of its Hearts, and then they drop all at once. Take three of a four-Heart squad's Hearts and you have scored nothing.

**The converse is just as important.** A destroyed squad drops its Stars whether or not its Stronghold is cleared. You do **not** have to clear a Stronghold to take Stars from it — clearing matters only for the Conquest Reward and for opening the path onward.

**Why it's distinctive:**

- **No troop losses.** Troops are provided by the system at your highest unlocked troop level and Fire Crystal level for each type. Injured troops on both sides recover after every battle.
- **Everything locks Thursday.** Heroes, formation, troop ratio, Stronghold assignment, and every stat bonus freeze at the end of the Preparation Phase.
- **The same squad attacks and defends.** There is no separate attack lineup. A squad built purely to tank will be a poor attacker, and vice versa.
- **Attacks are a limited budget, not a cooldown.** Each squad gets a fixed number of attacks for the whole Battle Phase, and unspent attempts bank.
- **Defenders get weaker as they win** — but only down to a floor. Morale decay lets numbers wear down a stronger squad, within limits.

### The Seven-Day Cycle

All times UTC.

| Day | Phase | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Monday 00:00 – Tuesday 23:00 | Preview | View your alliance's ranking on the event page |
| Tuesday 23:00 – 24:00 | Matchmaking | You must be in your alliance during this hour to participate |
| Wednesday 00:00 – Thursday 23:59 | Preparation | Set squads, heroes, formations, troop ratios, Stronghold assignments, and Wardens |
| Friday 00:00 – 23:59 | Scout | View enemy Stronghold garrisons. No attacks |
| **Saturday 00:00 – 23:59** | **Battle** | **Automatic defense plus your attack budget** |
| Sunday 00:00 – 23:59 | Results | Results and rewards |

::: warning You commit blind, and there is no way around it
Enemy power, enemy Stars and enemy garrisons all read as **???** for the entire Preparation Phase. They become visible on Friday — *after* Thursday's lock. **Scouting can never inform your garrison placement.** Build placement to be robust against any opponent rather than optimal against a guess, and treat anything that depends on knowing the enemy as a standing policy decided before the week begins.
:::

### Eligibility and Matchmaking

- **Alliance:** top 20 by power in the state when matchmaking begins.
- **Personal:** you must be in an eligible alliance when matchmaking begins, and your Furnace must reach **Lv. 25** during the event. If your Furnace is below 25 at matchmaking, you can still join after upgrading during the event.
- Groups are divided by server progress, and opponents with similar Star Ratings are matched first inside a group. **Matchmaking does not appear to pair on deployed squad power** — a heavily-participating alliance can be matched against one fielding a handful of squads.
- A matchmaking **bye (Auto-Advance)** counts as a win, settled after the Matchmaking Phase.
- Tier rewards are based on your alliance's tier **at the start of the event**, not the finish.
- A **Win Streak** means winning two consecutive events, and pays better than a single win.

**Changing alliance mid-event.** A Chief who moves to a different alliance during the event cannot participate — **unless they return to their original alliance.** What happens to squads already placed depends on when you leave:

| Leave during | What happens to your squads |
|---|---|
| Preparation | Automatically removed |
| Scout | Troops fully kept |
| Battle | Squads fully retained |

Rewards go to whoever is in the alliance **at the time of the reward tally**.

### Tier Movement and Rewards

Tier change depends on your current tier, not just the result:

| Tier | Win Streak | Victory | Loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rookie | +4 | +2 | **+1** |
| Stalwart | +4 | +2 | **+1** |
| Pioneer | +3 | +2 | **0** |
| Exalted | +2 | +1 | **0** |
| Mythic | +2 | +1 | **−1** |
| Legendary | +2 | +1 | **−1** |

::: warning "You climb even if you lose" is only true at the bottom
At Rookie and Stalwart a loss still advances you. From **Pioneer** upward a loss pays **nothing**, and from **Mythic** upward it **costs** you. Relegation is real above Exalted.

Two things still pay on a loss at every tier: the **loss rewards themselves**, roughly half a victory's, and **Stronghold Conquest Rewards**, which are independent of the result entirely.
:::

**Personal leaderboards.** Four boards run during the event — **Carnage** (damage dealt), **Star Plucker** (enemy Stars removed), **Wins** (attacker victories), and **Fighting Spirit** (total enemy Morale reduced). A **Star Allies** screen names the leader in each category **across both alliances**, and a **Personal Tally** screen reports all four for you at the close.

**Point weaker members at Fighting Spirit.** It scores *losing* attacks, so a member who cannot beat anything on the board still has a metric they can top — and those losing attacks are what make the alliance's kills possible. See The Grind Is Scored, below.

## The Board — read it every event

**The map is not fixed.** Across observed events it has appeared as **four Strongholds in two types** and as **six Strongholds in three types**. Star values, Heart counts and garrison capacity have all changed between events too. **Open Stronghold Info in-game every event and rebuild your numbers from it.**

The most recent observed board:

| | Empowered | Advanced | Primitive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stars per normal squad | **3** | **2** | **1** |
| Stars per Warden | **6** | **4** | **2** |
| Hearts, normal squad | **4** | **4** | **4** |
| Hearts, Warden | **5** | **5** | **5** |
| Garrison capacity | **20** | **20** | **20** |

**The Warden carries double the normal Star value and one extra Heart** in every Stronghold type. That is a tidier rule than earlier events showed, and it may or may not be general — check it.

**Deriving the Warden bonus without a second screen.** A Stronghold's Star total decomposes as `(normal squad count × normal value) + (Warden value)`. If the header total is higher than the count-derived figure, the difference is the live Warden bonus, readable directly.

**Garrison capacity can bind.** At 20 slots, a Stronghold you are deliberately stacking will fill. When more squads are assigned than the cap allows, the Warden and the highest-power squads get priority and the rest are rejected from the garrison.

::: tip Rejected does not mean benched
A squad that fails to garrison still gets its full attack budget on Saturday. It contributes no Stars and carries no Star risk, but it is a full attacker. In a large alliance, the overflow squads are your offensive reserve, not wasted deployments.
:::

### The Attack Path

Strongholds are connected. **You must defeat every garrison squad in a Stronghold before you can attack the Strongholds connected beyond it.** This is the same gating idea as Canyon Clash: the map is a network, not a menu.

The observed six-Stronghold layout is a funnel — three Primitive Strongholds on the front line, two Advanced behind them, one Empowered at the back, with a lateral link joining the two Advanced Strongholds. The centre Primitive opens **both** Advanced Strongholds.

Two consequences:

- **Your weakest Stronghold is your front door.** A thin front-line garrison is not a small loss, it is an opening to everything behind it.
- **Clearing a Stronghold means killing every squad in it**, not damaging them. There is no partial progress toward opening a path.

::: danger Read the connection graph before you plan placement
A Stronghold's job is set by its **position**, not by its Star value. A front-line Stronghold holding your strongest squads for the lowest Star payout looks like a misallocation on a Stars-per-power reading — and is not one if it is the door everything else sits behind. Never build a fill plan before you have looked at which Strongholds connect to which.
:::

### Undefended Strongholds — the Phaethon Guard

A Stronghold with no garrisoned squads at the start of the Scout Phase is filled by a system-generated squad called the **Phaethon Guard**. It takes the Warden slot, carries the full Warden Heart count and the full Warden Star value for that Stronghold type, and has very low power.

Three things follow:

- **An undefended enemy Stronghold is cheap, not free.** It still costs a full Warden's worth of Hearts to clear and it still pays its Warden Star value.
- **An alliance that barely turns out still has a Star total.** A board of auto-filled Strongholds is the sum of six Warden values, not zero.
- **Letting your own auto-fill fire is still a mistake**, just a smaller one than losing the Stronghold's Stars entirely. A very low-power Warden is an easy door.

## Preparation Phase

### Squad Deployment

Each Chief deploys up to two squads to friendly Strongholds. Two squads may sit in the same Stronghold. **These squads do not consume any March Queues.**

**Assigning heroes.** Three heroes must be assigned to each squad, and **the two squads cannot share heroes** — you need **six distinct heroes you already own** to field both. Check this before Thursday; a member short of six heroes cannot field a legal second squad.

**Assigning troops.** Troops are supplied automatically at your highest unlocked level and Fire Crystal level for each type. You choose the mix, and **the total must meet the maximum Deployment Capacity** — you fill it, you do not choose to under-fill it.

**R4 and R5 can move any member's squads between Strongholds** during Preparation. This is the main leadership lever in the whole event — do not leave placement to whoever logged in first.

**Automatic fallbacks at the start of the Scout Phase:**

- A Stronghold with no garrisoned squads gets one Phaethon Guard.
- A Stronghold with no appointed Warden has its most powerful squad appointed automatically.

Both fallbacks are worse than a real decision. Neither should ever fire.

### What Counts and What Does Not

These bonuses **are** included in battlefield calculations:

Troops · Hero Levels · Hero Star Levels · Hero Skills · Hero Gear · Hero Exclusive Gear **stats** · global Jeronimo and Natalia bonuses (these apply if you own the hero, even when not deployed) · Pet Skills (active by default; manual activation does not stack) · Pet Stats, Levels, Advancements, Refinements · Chief Charms · Chief Gear · Tech · Fire Crystal Tech · Flame Tech · Alliance Tech · Daybreak Island · Skins · VIP Level · Buildings · Facility (**must be active during squad setup**) · Expert Level, Relationship Advancements, Research, and Skills.

These bonuses **do not** apply:

Hero Exclusive Gear **Skills** · Territory bonuses · State Positions · President Skills · City Bonuses · Frostdragon Tyrant titles.

::: warning Two easy points to miss
Exclusive Gear contributes its stats but not its skill, so an Exclusive Gear skill that reads well on paper does nothing here. And the alliance Facility bonus only counts if it is **active while you set your squad** — leadership should confirm facility buffs are held and running across Wednesday and Thursday, not just on battle day.
:::

Squad power recalculates in real time while the Preparation Phase is open, and the bonuses in effect at the end of that phase are final. If squad capacity changes after deployment, troops are redistributed automatically using the ratio you originally chose.

**Expect a little drift.** Squad power moves between a mid-week snapshot and the Thursday lock as bonuses accrue. If a squad sits within about 1% of a threshold that matters — a Warden cutoff, a finisher cutoff — re-read it off the live screen before relying on it.

### Wardens

Each Stronghold can appoint **one Warden Squad**. Warden appointments can be changed at any time during the Preparation Phase.

- **Only the top 5 squads by power in that Stronghold are eligible.** You cannot appoint a low-power favourite.
- A Warden gains **extra Stars and extra Hearts**.
- Warden marks and Star values differ between Stronghold types.

The extra Heart is what matters defensively. A Warden forces the attacker through one more full Morale-grind cycle before it dies — 25% more attacks than a normal squad, for double the Stars.

**Practical Warden rules:**

1. One Warden per Stronghold. Never leave the auto-appointment to fire.
2. Prefer a strong squad belonging to a player who will actually be online during the Battle Phase — Wardens still attack, and a Warden's attack budget is as valuable as anyone's.
3. Do not stack every strong player into one Stronghold. Strongholds are connected, and a cheap Stronghold is a doorway.
4. Rank is not a qualification. Appoint by squad power, not by title.

## Battle Phase

### The Attack Budget

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Starting attempts per squad | 3 |
| Restore rate | 1 every 2 hours |
| Maximum restored per squad | 7 |
| **Total per squad for the whole phase** | **10** |
| Total per Chief (two squads) | 20 |

Attempts **bank**. You can save them and spend them all at once, which is what makes coordinated timed waves possible. An alliance with 40 participants has roughly 800 attacks available across the day — that is the resource leadership is actually allocating.

Restoration completes about 14 hours into the 24-hour phase, so late-day attacking draws on banked attempts rather than fresh ones.

::: tip Banking is safe
A squad whose panel reads **Attacks: 0** and *"Recovery limit reached"* has simply spent its 10 attacks and exhausted its 7 restorations. The same message appears on undamaged, surviving squads, so it is **not** a sign that destruction forfeits anything. A destroyed squad does not lose attempts it had not spent. An earlier version of this page advised front-loading on the opposite assumption; that warning has been withdrawn.
:::

### Morale

Officially: **Squad Stats = Squad Stats at the end of the Preparation Phase × Morale.**

- Attacker Morale is fixed at **100%** and cannot drop.
- A **garrison** squad loses Morale each time it **wins** a defense.

| Current Morale | Drop per successful defense |
|---|---|
| Above 80% | 10 percentage points |
| 80% or below | 5 percentage points |
| At 60% | **No further drop — this is the floor** |

The ladder runs **100 → 90 → 80 → 75 → 70 → 65 → 60**, reaching the floor after **six successful defenses**. Maximum effective power reduction: **40%**.

**Morale resets to 100% when the squad loses a Heart but survives.**

::: danger The kill cycle is the core arithmetic of the event
Because Morale resets on every Heart lost, each Heart is a fresh grind. To take a four-Heart squad you must wear its Morale down four separate times. Attacks that lose are not wasted — they are the mechanism. This is why a coordinated alliance of ordinary accounts can remove a heavily invested defender that no single member could beat.
:::

### The 60% Wall — who can and cannot finish a target

Because Morale stops at 60%, a defender's effective power never falls below 60% of its listed power. That produces a hard threshold:

```
Finisher threshold = defending squad's power x 0.60
```

**A squad below that threshold can never land the killing blow on that defender**, no matter how long the alliance grinds. Put the other way: a defender more than about **1.67×** your best attacker is permanently unkillable.

Three things follow, and the third is the one alliances get wrong:

- **On offense, route around it.** If an unkillable squad guards a gate, that branch is closed and the plan goes the other way. Do not feed finishing attacks into it.
- **On defense, it is an opportunity.** A garrison squad more than 1.67× the enemy's best attacker holds its Stars for the whole event. Since the same squad attacks and defends, the enemy's best attacker is effectively their best squad.
- **⭐ Sub-threshold squads should still attack it.** The threshold governs who can *finish*, not who should *attack*. Every losing attack drops the defender's Morale one step and helps whoever lands the kill. **Never publish a threshold to your alliance without saying this in the same breath** — otherwise half your roster reads it as "stay away" and the grind never happens.

### Stronghold Conquest Rewards

Separate from Stars, and easy to miss: **when every squad in an enemy Stronghold has been destroyed, every participant in your alliance can claim a reward.** Each Stronghold is calculated separately, and the reward must be **claimed manually** from the Rewards screen.

| Enemy Stronghold cleared | Reward per participant (observed) |
|---|---|
| Empowered | 6 Fire Crystals, 6 purple chests, 6× 1hr speedups |
| Each Advanced | 4 Fire Crystals, 4 purple chests, 4× 1hr speedups |
| Each Primitive | 2 Fire Crystals, 2 purple chests, 2× 1hr speedups |

A full six-Stronghold clear pays **20 / 20 / 20** per participant.

**This pays out whether you win the event or lose it**, and at Pioneer tier and above — where a loss pays no tier movement at all — it is the main material reason to fight a losing matchup hard. It also doubles the focus-fire rule: a partially cleared Stronghold pays nothing.

### The Grind Is Scored

A losing attack is not a wasted attack. Every failed attempt drops the defender's Morale one step, and that reduction is credited to you on the **Fighting Spirit** leaderboard.

Two things follow:

- **Grinding widens the finisher pool — within limits.** The first two Morale steps cut a defender's effective power by 10 points each; steps three through six by 5 points each, to a cap of 40%. A target only three of your squads could beat becomes one five can beat. It never becomes one everybody can beat.
- **Your strongest attackers will sit at 0% on that board** — because every time they win, the defender loses a Heart and Morale resets to 100%. That is correct, not a failure. If your best squad is climbing the Fighting Spirit board, it is being spent on fights it cannot win.

### Winning

1. If one alliance reduces the enemy's Stronghold Stars to **zero first**, it wins immediately.
2. Otherwise, at the end of the Battle Phase, the side with **more remaining Stronghold Stars** wins.
3. If Star counts are equal, the side with **higher Total Troop Power among surviving squads in Strongholds** wins.
4. If that is also equal, the side that **reached that count later** wins.
5. **Special case:** if both sides had equal Stars before the Battle Phase and still have equal Stars after it, **both sides lose.**

::: warning Reaching zero first locks the win — it does not end the day
The victory banner appears the moment the opponent hits zero, potentially with many hours left on the clock. The Battle Phase keeps running and **your own Stars keep falling** if the enemy is still attacking. Do not stand your garrison down on a banked win.
:::

Rule 5 is the one people get caught by. A quiet Saturday against an evenly matched opponent is not a draw — it is a double loss. Doing nothing is never the safe option.

**Rule 3 is worth planning around.** Surviving squad power is a tiebreak, and the figure shown at the top of the event map is exactly that — **live surviving deployed power**, which falls through the day as your garrison takes losses. It is not your alliance power and not a fixed snapshot. Deliberately under-garrisoning to free up attackers now costs you Stars *and* tiebreak position.

### Sparring

Once every enemy Stronghold is at zero Stars, you can still spend attack attempts to **spar** with the squads inside. Sparring battles are fought at 100% Morale on both sides and **have no effect on the outcome of the event**.

After a rout this makes the remaining budget free: there is no risk in spending it, and it still scores on the personal leaderboards.

## Strategy

### Turn Out — It Decides the Event

**Participation, not power, is the variable that decides most Winter Sieges.** A deployed-power gap between two alliances decomposes into two factors:

```
deployed power gap = per-player quality gap x participant count gap
```

In practice the second term dominates. A gap that looks like a brutal power mismatch is usually a roster-size-and-turnout problem wearing a costume — and turnout is the half you can fix inside a week.

**Troops are free.** The game supplies the squads, nothing dies, nothing needs healing, and there is no resource cost to registering. **Every eligible member who sits out is a pure forfeit** of two squads, their Star contribution, and twenty attacks.

**Do the recruiting during the Preview phase**, Monday and Tuesday. It is the only window where the number can still be changed, and it is worth more than every tactical decision below it combined.

**Measure deployment on Friday, not Saturday.** The power figure on the event map is *surviving* power. A comparison taken mid-battle measures attrition, not commitment.

### Every Player

- **Deploy both squads during Preparation.** They are your Stars and your entire attack budget.
- **Make sure you own six heroes** — three per squad, no sharing.
- **Build squads that can attack and defend**, because they will do both. A pure tank wastes ten attacks; a glass cannon folds on defense.
- **Finish every upgrade before Thursday 23:59 UTC.** Gear, charms, pets, and expert progress all count, and all freeze at that moment.
- **Check that the alliance Facility bonus is active when you set your squad.**
- **Spend your attacks.** Ten per squad is generous and they do not carry to next month.

### Troop Ratio

Whiteout Survival does not publish an official best ratio for this event, and the squad you register has to survive defense and be useful on offense. The widely used all-purpose PvP starting point is a reasonable default if you have not tested your own:

| Purpose | Infantry / Lancers / Marksmen | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| General all-purpose (default) | 50 / 20 / 30 | Balanced durability and damage |
| Leaning attack | 50 / 10 / 40 or 45 / 20 / 35 | For overflow squads that will never garrison |
| Leaning defense | 60 / 20 / 20 | Only if the squad is certain to garrison and you accept weaker attacks |
| Marksman-heavy PvE ratios | Not recommended | Bear Trap ratios are not PvP defense ratios |

Because the ratio locks with everything else, avoid a pure tank build for any squad that also has to attack. See [Troop Ratios](/troops/ratios) for the underlying mechanics.

### R4 / R5 Coordinator

- **Look at the connection graph first.** Placement is decided by topology, then by Star value.
- **Placement is your job, not the members'.** You can move every squad. Do it before Thursday closes.
- **Fill the highest-value Stronghold to capacity**, and pull the bodies you need out of the front line **weakest-first** — the Star gain is the same whichever squad you move, and leaving your strongest squads where contact is certain is free.
- **Keep same-rank Strongholds even**, in body count and in power. The enemy must clear an entire Stronghold to advance, so what matters across a rank of parallel doors is the weakest one, not the total.
- **Appoint every Warden manually.** Eligibility is capped to the top 5 squads by power in that Stronghold, so choose from that pool and prefer players who will be online Saturday.
- **Use the Scout Phase properly.** Friday is a full day to read every enemy garrison, count Hearts, compute finisher thresholds, and decide the target order before anyone can attack.
- **Publish thresholds and tiers together, with the grind instruction attached.**
- **Concentrate attacks.** Assign specific targets in a specific order and hold members to them.

### Target Selection — Stars per Attack

Neither the cheapest kill nor the biggest payout is the right sort key. **The right key is Stars divided by attacks.**

Work it out per target with two numbers: `G`, the number of grinding losses your finisher needs before it can win, and the target's Heart count.

```
Cost to destroy a squad = Hearts x (G + 1)
Stars per attack        = the squad's Star value / that cost
```

Worked at the most recent board's values, assuming your attacker beats the target at full Morale (`G = 0`):

| Target | Attacks | Stars | Stars per attack |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wounded Empowered squad, 1 Heart left** | 1 | 3 | **3.00** |
| Wounded Advanced squad, 1 Heart left | 1 | 2 | 2.00 |
| **Empowered Warden** | 5 | 6 | **1.20** |
| Advanced Warden | 5 | 4 | 0.80 |
| Fresh Empowered normal squad | 4 | 3 | 0.75 |
| Fresh Advanced normal squad | 4 | 2 | 0.50 |
| Primitive Warden | 5 | 2 | 0.40 |
| Fresh Primitive normal squad | 4 | 1 | **0.25** |

**Four readings that follow directly:**

- **⭐ Finish before you start.** A wounded Empowered squad is twelve times a fresh Primitive one, and a wounded Empowered **Warden** at one Heart is six Stars for a single attack. Hearts do not regenerate, so by late in the Battle Phase the board fills with cheap finishes. **Scan for wounded squads before choosing any new target.** This single rule is worth more than any other instruction on the day.
- **Stronghold rank is worth more per attack than anything except finishing.** Attacks spent on front-line Strongholds beyond what opening a door requires are a 3:1 loss.
- **A wounded low-rank squad beats a fresh high-rank one.** Worth interrupting for.
- **An enemy Warden you can beat outright is the best prize on its Stronghold**, not the worst — 25% more attacks for double the Stars. A Warden only becomes expensive when its higher power forces a long grind. **Sort Wardens by `G`, not by the Warden flag.**

::: warning Correction
An earlier version of this page said the Empowered Warden was the worst-rate target in the centre and should be taken late. That was an artefact of the old Morale table and the old Heart counts. With four-Heart normal squads and a correctly computed `G`, the opposite is true at equal `G`.
:::

**Why attack front-line Strongholds at all?** To open a door and to bank a Conquest Reward. Beyond that, they are the lowest-value action available.

### Tier Your Attackers

Free-for-all attacking and rigid single-target focus are both wrong. Sort your roster by **what each squad can actually kill**, publish the tiers before the Battle Phase, and let members act without waiting for a call.

| Tier | Definition | Assignment |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1** | Beats the enemy's highest-value garrison at full Morale | **Highest-value Stronghold only.** Never spend a Tier 1 attack on the front line |
| **Tier 2** | Beats front-line squads but not the top ones | **Doors.** Take the weakest thing you can beat and open a path |
| **Tier 3** | Beats nothing currently standing | **Grind one named target.** Losses widen the finisher pool and score on Fighting Spirit |

Cut the tiers off real numbers from the Scout Phase, not intuition. **A tier list beats an ordered target list** because an alliance acts across 24 hours and many time zones — a list needs a coordinator awake to re-sequence it, while a tier list lets someone logging in at 3am act correctly on their own. Publish both, tiers first.

**Tier each squad, not each player.** A Chief's two squads often land in different tiers. Publish the power thresholds and tell members to check both squads separately, or the list gets read as a roster of people.

**Above all tiers sits the finishing rule.** A Tier 2 squad should finish a wounded high-value squad it can beat rather than start a fresh front-line one.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Damaging squads instead of killing them.** Stars only move when a squad loses every Heart. Half a kill is worth exactly zero.

2. **Planning placement before reading the connection graph.** A Stronghold's job is its position. The cheapest-looking garrison may be the door to everything.

3. **Publishing a finisher threshold without the grind instruction.** It reads as "stay away," and the losing attacks that make the kill possible never get thrown.

4. **Reusing last event's numbers.** Star values, Heart counts, garrison capacity, Stronghold count and Stronghold types have all changed between events. Open Stronghold Info every time.

5. **Assuming the enemy Warden is unbeatable — or that it is a bad target.** Compute `Hearts × (G + 1)` and decide from the number.

6. **Feeding finishing attacks into a target above the 60% wall.** If it is more than ~1.67× your best squad, no amount of grinding will kill it. Route around it.

7. **Registering a pure defense build.** The same squad has ten attacks to spend. A 60/30/10 tank throws them away.

8. **Missing the Thursday lock.** Everything freezes at 23:59 UTC Thursday. An upgrade finished Friday morning contributes nothing.

9. **Forgetting the Facility bonus must be live during squad setup.** It is the one bonus with a timing condition attached.

10. **Building around Hero Exclusive Gear skills.** The stats count. The skills do not.

11. **Letting the auto-Warden or the Phaethon Guard fire.** Both are failure states, just cheap ones.

12. **Standing down after the win locks.** Reaching zero first banks the result; your Stars keep falling.

13. **Playing passively while tied.** Equal Stars before and after the Battle Phase means **both** alliances lose.

14. **Chasing a State Title or President skill for the event.** State Positions, President Skills, City Bonuses, Territory bonuses, and Frostdragon Tyrant titles are all excluded.
