# Chief Charms

::: info
Quick take: Charms are the Furnace 25 layer that adds **Lethality and Health** to all your troops. They matter more than their size suggests, because Lethality is the one stat the rest of your account barely provides. Fill every one of the eighteen slots and bring them all to Level 4 before you push anything higher — those early levels return roughly five times the power per Charm Design that Level 7 and above do. The one-charm push to Level 11 is a late-account move, not a starting move. Charms are also one of the highest-scoring SvS prep tasks, so time the spend for the window.
:::

## What charms are

Chief Charms unlock at **Furnace 25** and attach to your Chief Gear. Each of your six gear pieces has **three charm slots**, for **eighteen charms** in total. A charm takes its troop type from the gear piece it sits on, and you cannot change it. Unlike Chief Gear, charms have **no set bonus**, so you are free to level them in any order.

What they give: **Troop Lethality and Health** for one troop type per charm, applied to all your marches automatically.

## Why charms punch above their weight

Your troop damage is roughly **Attack multiplied by Lethality.** The rest of your account — research, heroes, buildings, Chief Gear — pours in plenty of Attack and Defense, but very little Lethality and Health. Charms are the main source of those two scarce stats, so a level of charm often buys more real combat power than the same effort spent on a stat you already have in surplus. That is the case for not treating charms as an afterthought once they unlock.

## The slot map

Charms inherit their troop type from the gear piece, matching your [Chief Gear](/strategy/gear/chief-gear) layout:

| Troop Type | Gear Pieces | Charms |
| ---------- | ----------- | ------ |
| **Infantry** | Coat + Pants | 6 (Health & Lethality) |
| **Marksman** | Ring + Weapon (Cane) | 6 (Health & Lethality) |
| **Lancer** | Cap (Helmet) + Watch | 6 (Health & Lethality) |

## How leveling works

Charms go from **Level 1 to 16.** From Level 4 on, each major level is split into sub-levels you clear in sequence:

- Levels 4–10: four sub-levels each (4 → 4.1 → 4.2 → 4.3 → 5)
- Levels 11–16: five sub-levels each (10 → 10.1 → 10.2 → 10.3 → 10.4 → 11)

Levels 1–11 need only **Charm Guides** and **Charm Designs**. Levels 12–16 add **Jewel Secrets**, the rarest charm material, and are gated behind your state's age. Community sources disagree on exactly where that gate sits — some report the whole 12–16 band opening at **Generation 7**, others report only Level 16 gated there with a Level 15 cap below it. Either way, if your state is young, plan around Level 11 as your working ceiling.

## What a level costs and what it returns

This is the part most charm advice skips, and it changes the whole strategy. Charm Designs are the bottleneck material — you always have fewer than you need. So the question that matters is **power gained per Charm Design spent.**

Per charm, per level:

| Level | Guides | Designs | Power per Design |
| ----- | ------ | ------- | ---------------- |
| 1 | 5 | 0 | free (205,700 power) |
| 2 | 40 | 15 | ~5,500 |
| 3 | 60 | 40 | ~2,050 |
| 4 | 80 | 100 | ~820 |
| 5 | 100 | 200 | ~620 |
| 6 | 120 | 300 | ~415 |
| 7 | 140 | 400 | ~310 |
| 8 | 200 | 400 | ~310 |
| 9 | 300 | 400 | ~310 |
| 10 | 420 | 420 | ~295 |
| 11 | 560 | 420 | ~295 |

Two things fall out of that table.

**The early levels are a cliff.** Taking one slot from Level 1 to Level 4 costs 155 Designs and returns about 246,000 power — roughly **1,590 power per Design.** One level from 7 to 8 costs 400 Designs and returns 124,000 — about **310 per Design.** The early levels are worth about **five times as much per Design.** Level 1 itself costs no Designs at all, so any slot you have left empty is free power sitting on the table.

**Above Level 6 the curve is flat.** Every level from 7 to 11 returns 295–310 power per Design. That means the long-running argument about whether to level charms evenly or concentrate them is, above Level 6, close to meaningless — the two approaches land within a few percent of each other. Do not spend energy optimizing a decision the numbers do not care about. Spend it on the early levels, where the difference is real.

## Upgrade strategy

1. **Gear quality first.** Charm bonuses scale with the quality of the gear they sit on, so a solid Chief Gear floor makes every charm worth more. If you are still crafting gear pieces, do that first — but if you have charm materials sitting idle while you wait on gear materials, spend them, since the two use completely separate resources.
2. **Fill all eighteen slots.** Level 1 costs five Charm Guides and no Designs. An unfilled slot is the single clearest waste in the system.
3. **Bring all eighteen to Level 4.** This is the highest-return spending available anywhere in the charm system. Do it before pushing any single charm higher, including charms on troop types you rarely field — at this price the power is worth having regardless.
4. **Then Levels 5 and 6 across the board**, still well above the flat zone.
5. **Above Level 6, weight to the troops you actually field.** There is no set bonus rewarding uniformity: a marksman-line account leans marksman charms (lethality is your damage); an infantry-heavy PvP or Alliance Championship build leans infantry (survivability). Just do not expect the weighting to matter much — the per-Design returns are nearly identical across slots up here.
6. **Level 11 across all eighteen is the eventual free-to-play plateau.** It is the last tier before Jewel Secrets. Getting there is a long project, not a near-term target.

A balancing tip: you do not have to win every stat through charms. If your charms lean infantry, you can lean your [research](/strategy/research) or hero gear toward marksman, so your overall stats end up balanced relative to what you field.

## The Level 11 material exchange: a late-account move

A common piece of advice is to push one charm to Level 11 as early as possible, because reaching Level 11 unlocks the **Chief Charm Material Exchange** — a shop that trades your surplus materials into the ones you are short on, most usefully **two Charm Guides for one Charm Design.**

That advice is sound on a mature account and **wrong on a young one.** Here is why.

**The exchange has nothing to convert until you have a Guide surplus.** Climbing one charm from Level 1 to Level 11 consumes about **2,695 Charm Designs but only 2,025 Charm Guides.** You spend more of the scarce material than the common one on the way up. The Guide mountain that makes the exchange powerful — players report five-figure Guide stockpiles on old accounts — builds up over a long period when income keeps delivering Guides faster than you can spend them. On a young state that surplus does not exist yet, so unlocking the shop early buys you access to a trade you cannot make.

**The detour is expensive.** Pushing a single charm from Level 7 to Level 11 costs about **1,640 Charm Designs** for roughly 496,000 power. Spent instead on early levels across your unfinished slots, those same 1,640 Designs return well over two million power. That is the trade you are actually making.

**The unlock condition may not even be available to you.** Sources conflict. The community wiki lists the requirement as simply getting any one charm to Level 11. At least one well-sourced community guide reports it as **two conditions at once** — any charm at Level 11 **and** your state at Generation 7. This has not been settled publicly.

::: warning
Before committing materials to a Level 11 push, open your charm screen and read the requirement text on the locked exchange itself. It takes seconds and it decides whether the push is worth anything to you right now. If it is gated on your state's generation, the push buys nothing until that generation arrives.
:::

**The practical rule:** treat Level 11 as a project you start when your Charm Guides comfortably outnumber your Charm Designs in the bag, and when your state is within sight of the generation gate. Around Gen 5 is a reasonable point to begin funneling Designs into one charm so it is ready when the exchange becomes useful. Before that, every Design goes into raising your floor.

## Materials

| Material | Role | Main sources |
| -------- | ---- | ------------ |
| **Charm Guides** | Common; needed at every level (large amounts late) | Frostfire Mine, Castle Battle, Foundry / Power Region / Canyon shops, events |
| **Charm Designs** | The real bottleneck; needed at every level | Frostfire Mine, Castle Battle, the same shops, and the **Giant Elk pet** via Lighthouse Intel Missions |
| **Jewel Secrets** | Rare; only for Levels 12–16 | Frostfire Mine, Castle Battle, high-tier event and shop rewards |

The [Frostfire Mine](/events/frostfire-mine) and Castle Battle ([Sunfire Castle](/events/sunfire-castle)) are your steady supply. Whenever a shop or event reward offers a choice between Guides and Designs, **take Designs** — that is the material that gates you. If you are pushing charms seriously, the Giant Elk pet is worth investing in for its passive Charm Design income.

Because Designs are the constraint and your income of them is fixed, the honest read on charms for a young free-to-play account is that they are a **slow drip, not a lever you can pull.** Raise the floor cheaply, then let the materials accumulate and spend them in scoring windows. Fire Crystal upgrades, research, and troop tier will move your account faster in the meantime.

## Bank it for SvS

Charm upgrades are one of the highest-scoring tasks in State of Power prep, and the points climb sharply at higher charm levels. That makes charms a prime thing to **bank**: when you have a charm level ready and prep is close, hold it for the window. You get the permanent Lethality and Health either way — and during prep you also get the points that decide which state wins the buff and attacks first. See the [SvS Prep Checklist](/events/svs/prep-checklist) for the scoring days. King of Icefield and Alliance Mobilization reward the same upgrades, so overlapping windows let one spend score more than once.

This is the discipline that wins states: not hoarding charm materials forever, but timing the **spend** to land inside prep.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving slots empty or at Level 1.** Level 1 costs no Charm Designs at all. Every unfilled slot is free power you are not taking.
- **Chasing Level 11 on a young state.** The material exchange only pays once you hold a real Charm Guide surplus, and the climb itself consumes more Designs than Guides. Raise your floor first.
- **Agonizing over even-versus-concentrated above Level 6.** The per-Design returns are within a few percent of each other up there. It is not the decision that is costing you.
- **Maxing charms on low-quality gear.** The charm scales with gear quality — raise the gear floor so the charms pay off.
- **Dumping charm levels the moment you can afford them,** outside an SvS window. Same stats, but you throw away the prep points.

## Quick reference

- Unlocks at Furnace 25 · 18 charms · 3 per gear piece · troop type fixed by slot · **no set bonus and no uniformity bonus.**
- Charms give Lethality and Health — the stats nothing else provides much of.
- Charm Designs are the bottleneck. Judge every spend by power per Design.
- Levels 1–4 return roughly five times as much per Design as Levels 7+. Fill all eighteen slots and take them all to Level 4 first.
- Above Level 6 the return curve is flat — even versus concentrated barely matters.
- Level 11 unlocks the material exchange, but the exchange only helps once Guides outnumber Designs in your bag. Start the push around Gen 5, not before. Check the in-game unlock text; sources disagree on whether a generation gate applies.
- Gear quality first; charm bonus scales with it.
- Bank charm levels for SvS prep — top scorer, and you would spend them anyway.
